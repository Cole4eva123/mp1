Pingyang

A county of 1,042 square kilometres that runs from the tidal reach of the Aojiang out to fifty-two islands in the East China Sea. Settled since the Neolithic, a county since 283 CE, under this name since 914.

At a Glance

A County on the Edge of the East China Sea

Pingyang sits on the southern coast of Zhejiang, inside the prefecture of Wenzhou. Cangnan lies to the south, Wencheng and Taishun to the west, Rui’an to the north, and the open sea to the east. The county government sits at Kunyang; the river port of Aojiang, ten minutes away, carries its shipping.

  • 1,042 km² of land
  • 283 CE, first county seat
  • 52 islands over 500 m²
  • 1,876 marine species recorded

The Name

The county began in 283 CE as Shiyang and was renamed Hengyang soon after. In 914 the Wuyue king Qian Liu put down the Hengyang revolt and renamed the place for the outcome — heng yang ji ping, “Hengyang pacified.” The name has stood for more than 1,100 years.

The Shipyard

In 239 CE the kingdom of Eastern Wu built the Hengyu boat garrison here, one of its three shipbuilding bases: a war-fleet yard and a naval training ground in one, staffed partly with exiles sent down from the capital.

The Schools

The county opened a school the year it was founded — the first local government school in Zhejiang. It produced 706 imperial graduates, and in the Southern Song, fifteen top graduates of the military examinations, which earned it the label “hometown of military champions.”

30 Nautical Miles Offshore

The Nanji Archipelago

  • Dasha’ao, the island’s south-facing bay, from the north

  • Pearl Beach, inside the national marine reserve

  • Mei-ling House, Dongfang village, Nanji town

Fifty-two islands larger than 500 square metres, plus 55 exposed rocks and 14 submerged ones, sit 30 nautical miles off the mouth of the Aojiang. The land adds up to 11.13 km²; the water around it, 201.6 km². Nanji Island itself covers 7.64 km² and rises to 229.1 metres at Dashan. One islet in the group, Daotiao Shan, is a base point of China’s territorial sea — the only one in Wenzhou.

The State Council made the archipelago a national marine nature reserve in September 1990, one of the first five in the country, and UNESCO added it to the World Network of Biosphere Reserves in December 1998. Surveys have logged 1,876 marine species here, which is why it is called the Kingdom of Shells and Algae.

North Harbour — Upstream of Aojiang

The Inland Half

Above the tideline the county turns to hills. Shuitou, the second economic centre, is the eastern gate of the Nanyandang national scenic area; Shunxi and Shanmen sit further up the valleys, and Qingjie is a She ethnic township. Two upper branches of the Aojiang — the Shuitou and the Tengjiao — meet here in a fork the old verses compare to a tree.

Twice Daily, Strongest in Autumn

The River Runs Backwards

The Aojiang is one of the few rivers in China that carries a tidal bore: a wall of seawater that pushes upstream against the current on a strong tide. Aojiang town and the port of Qiancang both grew on that tide, which set the working day for every boat on the river.

Practical

If You Go

Getting There

Pingyang station sits on the Wenzhou–Fuzhou line, about an hour from Wenzhou South. Boats to Nanji leave from Aojiang and cross roughly 55 km of open water; sailings depend on the weather, so build a spare day into the plan.

When to Go

Late April and early May bring black-tailed gulls to the outer islands and peach blossom to Yong’an. Typhoon season runs from July to September and closes the ferries. Autumn gives the strongest bores and the clearest water.

What to Drink

Pingyang Huangtang is a yellow tea, made here and in the neighbouring counties for more than two centuries. Look for the leaf the growers describe as white-hearted and yellow-leafed; it brews thinner and sweeter than a green.

Who Came From Here

Su Buqing (1902–2003) was born at Daixi in Tengjiao, took his doctorate at Tohoku in 1931, founded China’s school of differential geometry, led Fudan University from 1978 and started the Chinese Annals of Mathematics in 1980.

Kunyang · Longshan · 1878–1884

Wenming Pagoda

From the Song to the Ming, Pingyang produced enough scholars to be called the little Zou-Lu of the southeast. By the late Qing that run had dried up, and local gentry led by Chen Jizhong asked the magistrate Tang Zhaoxi for a pagoda to restore the county’s literary fortune. Work began in 1878 and finished in the third month of 1884.

Because each storey steps inward, the tower tapers like an upright brush, and locals called it the Writing-brush Pagoda long before the official name stuck. The wooden stair inside burned during the Republican period and was never replaced.

Qiancang · South Face

Baosheng Temple Pagodas

The two pagodas stand at the old temple site above Qiancang, a river town on the Aojiang whose name — money granary — records what it once stored and shipped. The temple itself is gone; the pair of towers, and the carved rock faces on the hill behind them, are what remains of the town’s religious quarter.

Qiancang sat on the road and the river at once, which made it the natural gathering point for anything moving between the coast and the upper valleys — pilgrims, salt, and in 1861, rebels.

Qiancang · Fengshan

Shake Rock

A boulder balanced on Fengshan hill so finely that it rocks under a push and settles back. Its north face carries an inscription, one of the group known collectively as the Qiancang cliff carvings — the local habit of signing the landscape rather than a wall.

The carvings are read from the path, which climbs from the town through the temple terraces towards the twin pagodas.

Qiancang · 1861–1862

Lingshun Temple Site

Also called North Hill Temple, this terrace is where the Golden Coin Society gathered before the rising of 1861. Wenzhou had taken a decade of typhoons, floods, plague and famine; the county granary at Pingyang had fallen from 8,634 piculs of grain in 1851 to 45 by 1859, while the government kept levying contributions to pay for the war against the Taiping.

What began as a quarrel between two local men became a rebellion. The society took Fuding and Pingyang, attacked Wenzhou and besieged Rui’an before imperial troops put it down the following year.
